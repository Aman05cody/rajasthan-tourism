Dilwara Temples

Marble poetry — India's finest Jain temples — Mount Abu, Rajasthan

About this site

Hidden among the mango trees of Mount Abu, the Dilwara temples look modest from outside — and then the doors open onto some of the most exquisite marble carving on earth.

The Vimala Vasahi (1031), dedicated to the first Tirthankara Rishabhanatha, and the Luna Vasahi (1230), dedicated to Neminatha, are carved from white marble so fine it seems translucent: ceilings of concentric carved domes, lotus pendants hanging like stone lace, and processions of elephants, dancers and deities covering every pillar.

History

The temples were built between the 11th and 13th centuries under the Chaulukya (Solanki) rulers of Gujarat, funded by their Jain ministers — Vimal Shah first, then the brothers Vastupala and Tejpala, who also built temples at Ranakpur.

Craftsmen are said to have been paid in gold by weight of marble dust removed — a measure of how much stone they carved away to create the lace-like interiors.

The temples remain active Jain pilgrimage sites, managed by the Seth Shri Kalyanji Anandji trust; photography is not allowed inside, which has only added to their mystique.
