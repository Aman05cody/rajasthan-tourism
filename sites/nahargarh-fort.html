Nahargarh Fort

The Tiger Fort with Jaipur's finest sunset view — Jaipur, Rajasthan

About this site

Nahargarh Fort crowns the Aravalli ridge directly above Jaipur, its walls running along the hilltop like a stone crown. 'Nahargarh' means 'abode of tigers', and the fort was as much a royal retreat as a defensive post.

Inside, the Madhavendra Bhawan — a courtyard of suites built for the queens — glows with restored frescoes, colourful corridors and delicate mirror work. From the ramparts, especially at sunset, the whole of Jaipur spreads out below in a haze of pink.

History

Sawai Jai Singh II built the fort in 1734 as part of Jaipur's ring of hill defences, together with Amber and Jaigarh. Legend says the restless spirit of a Rathore prince, Nahar Singh Bhomia, haunted the construction until a temple was raised to him within the walls.

In 1868 Sawai Ram Singh II added the Madhavendra Bhawan palace for his queens. Unlike Amber or Jaigarh, Nahargarh never faced an attack — its guns stayed silent through every war.

Today it is Jaipur's favourite sunset point, and its ramparts appear in films and music videos shot in the Pink City.
