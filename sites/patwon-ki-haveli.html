Patwon Ki Haveli

The grandest merchant mansion of the Golden City — Jaisalmer, Rajasthan

About this site

Patwon Ki Haveli was the first — and remains the grandest — of Jaisalmer's merchant mansions: not one house but a cluster of five havelis rising five storeys in golden sandstone.

Its facades are a honeycomb of over sixty carved balconies (jharokhas), each different, with mirror-work ceilings, painted walls and filigree screens so fine they look woven rather than carved. The Patwas traded in gold and silver brocade, and their wealth is written all over the stone.

History

Guman Chand Patwa, one of Jaisalmer's richest traders, commissioned the haveli in 1805; his five sons each added their own mansion, which is why it is a cluster rather than a single house. Construction stretched over some fifty years.

Like the fort above it, the haveli grew rich on the Silk Road caravan trade — and declined when the trade routes shifted to the sea under the British.

Parts of the complex now house government offices and a museum of Jaisalmer's brocade and craft traditions, while the facades remain the most photographed street in the old city.
